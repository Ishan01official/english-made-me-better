# Professional English: 150 Real Work Examples

This file teaches practical English for emails, ticket updates, worknotes, meetings, interviews, and technical explanations.

## First Principles Of Professional English

Professional English is not complicated English. It is clear English.

Good professional messages have:

- Context: what this is about.
- Action: what happened or what is needed.
- Reason: why it matters.
- Next step: what should happen now.
- Tone: polite, direct, and calm.

## Core Formula

```text
Context + Action + Reason + Next Step
```

Example:

> I reviewed the deployment logs. The failure happened during authentication. Could you please confirm whether the service principal has the required permissions?

## Worknote Templates

### Investigation Started

Checked the initial details shared in the ticket. The issue appears to be related to access or configuration. Requested additional logs and timestamp from the customer for further validation.

### Logs Reviewed

Reviewed the logs for the reported time period. The logs show authentication failures during service startup. Next step is to verify permissions and confirm whether any recent configuration changes were made.

### Customer Action Needed

We need the customer to share the required logs, error timestamp, and resource details. Further investigation depends on these details.

### Issue Resolved

The issue has been resolved after updating the configuration. The service is running successfully now. Customer verification is pending.

## Email Templates

### Asking For Details

Subject: Request for Additional Details

Hi Team,

Could you please share the required logs, error timestamp, and affected resource details? These details will help us verify the issue and identify the next step.

Regards,
Ishan

### Follow-Up

Subject: Follow-Up On Requested Details

Hi Team,

I am following up to check whether you were able to share the requested logs and timestamp. We will continue the investigation once we receive those details.

Regards,
Ishan

## Interview Answer Pattern

Use STAR:

- Situation: What was happening?
- Task: What did you need to do?
- Action: What did you do?
- Result: What improved?

Example:

> In my previous task, a deployment failed because of a configuration issue. My responsibility was to identify the failure point and update the ticket clearly. I reviewed the logs, found the authentication error, and shared the required next steps with the team. As a result, the issue was resolved faster and the communication became clearer.
