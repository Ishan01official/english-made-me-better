# Recommended English Books

These books are recommended resources only. This repo does not copy their content.

## Grammar

### English Grammar in Use by Raymond Murphy

- Best for: self-study grammar with clear examples.
- Level: beginner to intermediate.
- How to use: study one unit, write your own examples, then explain the rule simply.
- Weekly plan: 3 units per week.
- Practice method: write 10 original sentences per unit.
- What not to do: do not only read answers; use the grammar in speaking.

### Practical English Usage by Michael Swan

- Best for: checking confusing grammar and usage questions.
- Level: intermediate to advanced.
- How to use: search one doubt, read the explanation, write 3 examples.
- Weekly plan: 5 small topics per week.
- Practice method: make a personal mistake list.
- What not to do: do not read it like a novel.

### Wren & Martin High School English Grammar and Composition

- Best for: traditional grammar foundation and exercises.
- Level: beginner to intermediate.
- How to use: use it for grammar names, rules, and structured exercises.
- Weekly plan: 2 chapters per week.
- Practice method: convert rules into your own examples.
- What not to do: do not memorize definitions without writing sentences.

## Vocabulary

### Word Power Made Easy by Norman Lewis

- Best for: word roots and vocabulary building.
- Level: intermediate.
- How to use: learn roots, then use words in your own sentences.
- Weekly plan: 2 sessions per week.
- Practice method: create word families and collocations.
- What not to do: do not memorize isolated words only.

### Oxford Word Skills

- Best for: practical everyday and academic vocabulary.
- Level: beginner to advanced, depending on edition.
- How to use: study one topic group and speak using those words.
- Weekly plan: 3 topics per week.
- Practice method: write mini-dialogues.
- What not to do: do not skip pronunciation.

## Pronunciation

### Ship or Sheep?

- Best for: English sound contrast practice.
- Level: beginner to intermediate.
- How to use: practice minimal pairs aloud.
- Weekly plan: 3 sound contrasts per week.
- Practice method: record and compare.
- What not to do: do not rush sounds before your mouth position is correct.

### English Pronunciation in Use

- Best for: pronunciation, stress, rhythm, and intonation.
- Level: beginner to advanced, depending on edition.
- How to use: study one unit and repeat the audio pattern.
- Weekly plan: 3 units per week.
- Practice method: shadow short sentences.
- What not to do: do not practice silently.

## Writing

### The Elements of Style

- Best for: concise writing.
- Level: intermediate.
- How to use: apply one principle to your own paragraph.
- Weekly plan: 1 short section per week.
- Practice method: rewrite long sentences clearly.
- What not to do: do not treat every style rule as absolute.

### On Writing Well

- Best for: clarity and nonfiction writing.
- Level: intermediate to advanced.
- How to use: study one idea and rewrite one paragraph.
- Weekly plan: 1 chapter per week.
- Practice method: remove weak words and unclear phrases.
- What not to do: do not only admire the writing; edit your own writing.

### Oxford Guide to Plain English

- Best for: professional, simple, reader-friendly writing.
- Level: intermediate to advanced.
- How to use: apply it to emails, notes, and documentation.
- Weekly plan: 1 principle per week.
- Practice method: rewrite work messages.
- What not to do: do not make writing complicated to sound smart.

## Speaking

### Speak English Like an American

- Best for: idioms and spoken expressions.
- Level: intermediate.
- How to use: learn expressions in context and use them in short dialogues.
- Weekly plan: 2 dialogues per week.
- Practice method: record yourself using expressions naturally.
- What not to do: do not force idioms into every sentence.

### English Conversation Practice Resources

- Best for: daily speaking fluency.
- Level: all levels.
- How to use: pick one topic, speak, listen, correct, repeat.
- Weekly plan: 5 speaking prompts per week.
- Practice method: 60-second answers and teach-back summaries.
- What not to do: do not wait until grammar is perfect before speaking.
